The ensemble

Seven models each predict every game. Their weights are fit on out-of-sample predictions only, and a model gets zero weight when the others already cover what it knows.

Brier score by season

Lower is better. Every season is predicted by a model trained only on earlier seasons.

Calibration

When the model says 70%, the home team should win about 70% of the time.

How it works

Data comes from nflverse play-by-play (2006 to now). Team stats only count plays in competitive situations (win probability 20 to 80%, or the first three quarters within 14 points), so garbage time does not distort them. No yards per game, points per game or raw turnover counts are used. Features include opponent-adjusted EPA per pass and rush (a weekly ridge regression on offense and defense), success rate, early-down EPA, explosive play rate, CPOE, depth of target, yards after catch per target, pressure rate and EPA under pressure, turnover-worthy pass and fumble rates, red-zone touchdown rate regressed to the league median, a starting quarterback rating and how it compares to his team, rest, travel miles, time-zone shifts, wind and cold. Recent games count more through a 7-game exponential moving average. Injuries count each player listed Out or Doubtful by his recent share of snaps, so a starting tackle matters and a backup does not. Backup and rookie quarterbacks start from a rating based on where they were drafted. Outdoor games get a kickoff weather forecast.

The models learn neutral-site team strength, then home field is added back as a fixed 1.2 to 1.8 points. Every season in the backtest is predicted only from earlier seasons and scored against sportsbook closing lines.

Projected scores come from a separate total-points model built on each team's recent scoring, efficiency and quarterback play, split between the teams using the predicted margin.

The predicted margin comes from the ensemble's win probability, scaled to points using past games. Spread contracts on Kalshi are priced the same way: the chance a team wins by more than the line, compared to the ask price after fees.

Kalshi edge is the model's chance minus the ask price minus Kalshi's taker fee. Positive means the model thinks that contract is cheap. Paper results only.
